# Nuclear Systems: Notes for High School Students

## 1. Introduction to the Atom
The atom is the building block of matter. It consists of:
*   **Protons:** Positively charged particles in the nucleus.
*   **Neutrons:** Neutral particles in the nucleus.
*   **Electrons:** Negatively charged particles orbiting the nucleus.

The **nucleus** contains almost all the mass of the atom but takes up very little space.

## 2. Isotopes and Stability
*   **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but a different number of neutrons. (e.g., Carbon-12 and Carbon-14).
*   **Stability:** A nucleus is stable if the strong nuclear force (which holds protons and neutrons together) overcomes the electrostatic repulsion between protons. If a nucleus has too many or too few neutrons, it becomes unstable and radioactive.

## 3. Radioactivity and Decay
Radioactive decay is the process by which an unstable nucleus releases energy and/or particles to reach a more stable state.

### Types of Decay:
*   **Alpha Decay ($\alpha$):** The nucleus emits an alpha particle (2 protons, 2 neutrons, essentially a Helium nucleus). Reduces atomic number by 2 and mass number by 4.
*   **Beta Decay ($\beta$):** A neutron turns into a proton and an electron (beta particle). The electron is ejected. Increases atomic number by 1; mass number stays the same.
*   **Gamma Decay ($\gamma$):** The nucleus releases high-energy electromagnetic radiation (gamma rays) to lose excess energy. No change in protons or neutrons.

## 4. Half-Life
Half-life is the time required for half of the radioactive atoms in a sample to decay.
*   **Example:** If a substance has a half-life of 10 years, after 10 years, 50% remains. After another 10 years (20 total), 25% remains.

## 5. Nuclear Energy: Fission vs. Fusion

### Nuclear Fission (Splitting)
*   The splitting of a heavy nucleus (like Uranium-235) into smaller nuclei.
*   Triggered by a neutron hitting the nucleus.
*   Releases a large amount of energy and more neutrons, which can trigger a **chain reaction**.
*   **Uses:** Nuclear power plants, atomic bombs.

### Nuclear Fusion (Joining)
*   The combining of two light nuclei (like Hydrogen isotopes) into a heavier nucleus (like Helium).
*   Requires extremely high temperatures and pressure.
*   Releases even more energy than fission.
*   **Where it happens:** The Sun and other stars.

## 6. Applications and Safety
*   **Medicine:** Cancer treatment (radiotherapy) and medical imaging (PET scans).
*   **Energy:** Clean energy source (no CO2 emissions), but carries risks of waste disposal and accidents.
*   **Safety:** Radiation can damage living tissue. Protection methods include shielding (lead/concrete), distance, and limiting exposure time.
